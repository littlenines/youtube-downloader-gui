# Downloads

1. The page sends `POST /api/downloads` with the user's choices.
2. `normalizeDownloadRequest` validates them into `DownloadOptions`, and the queue creates a `Job`.
3. When a slot is free (Settings → downloads at the same time), the queue waits for the tools and starts yt-dlp with `buildDownloadArguments`. The link always comes after `--`, so it can never be read as an option.
4. Each job gets its own partial folder, `<download folder>/.yt-downloader-partial/<job id>`, passed as `-P temp:`. yt-dlp keeps everything unfinished there (stream parts, fragments, thumbnails, merge and conversion output) and moves each file to the download folder only once it is complete.
5. yt-dlp prints marker lines that the app reads (`parseOutputLine`):
   - `VITEM`: an item starts, with its playlist position and title.
   - `VPROG`: progress, with the stream's video and audio codec, bytes, total, speed and seconds left.
   - `VFILE`: the final file after merging and conversion.
6. The codecs tell the streams apart: picture only, sound only, both in one stream, or neither (subtitles). For a video, the picture fills the progress bar to 85% and the sound the rest.

**What the list shows.** A download spends most of its time outside the actual download, so each job has an `activity` (`job-progress.ts`), and the list shows it:

| Activity | When | Shown |
|---|---|---|
| `preparing` | Waiting for the tools, reading the video page (3–8 s on YouTube, while yt-dlp solves its checks), and between a video's picture and sound | The step ("Getting the video info…") and a moving bar |
| `downloading` | A `VPROG` line arrived for a stream yt-dlp doesn't report as `finished` yet | The step, percent, "2.0 MB of 4.0 MB", speed and time left |
| `processing` | yt-dlp reported the last stream as `finished` (`progress.status`, the last field of `VPROG`). Not "downloaded ≥ total", because the total can be an estimate that a stream outgrows: converting or saving the audio (named per format, for example "Converting to MP3"), merging picture and sound, or a clip (whose progress yt-dlp only reports at the end, so a new item doesn't replace "Downloading the clip") | The step and a moving bar, without the last speed |

Music often downloads in a fraction of a second, faster than the list refreshes (every 0.7 s), while reading the page and converting take several seconds each, so the moving bar is what keeps those phases from looking stuck. A finished job also shows how long it took ("took 12s"): `activeSeconds` adds up each run, from `runStartedAt` to its end, so time spent paused or waiting for the next "Later" window doesn't count, and it's saved in `queue.json` across restarts. Progress that arrives after Pause or Cancel, while yt-dlp is still exiting, is ignored, and cancelling clears the activity, speed and time left. Subtitles clear the previous stream's numbers and show the moving bar. The moving bar sweeps across; with reduced motion turned on in the system, it pulses instead.
7. When the job ends in any way, its partial folder is deleted. On success the job is added to the history. On cancel the process tree is stopped (process group on Linux and macOS, `taskkill /T` on Windows), so nothing unfinished reaches the download folder at any stage.

A job keeps its slot until its yt-dlp process has exited, even after it was cancelled. Retrying a cancelled job queues it, and it starts only once the previous run has fully ended, so two runs never write the same file.

**Pausing and resuming.** **Pause** stops a download's yt-dlp process but keeps its partial folder, and the job becomes `paused`, which frees its slot. **Resume** queues it again: yt-dlp continues the `.part` files in the same partial folder instead of starting over (measured: a 4K download paused at 7% resumed at 7%). A clip is the exception, because its ffmpeg cut can't continue and starts again. A job remembers the download folder it started in, so changing the folder in Settings while it is paused doesn't lose its partial files. Partial files are deleted when a job is finished (done, failed or cancelled), or when its run was cancelled even if Retry was clicked before the process exited, so a retry always starts fresh. Pausing then resuming while the old process is still exiting keeps them. If yt-dlp finishes on its own just as Pause is clicked, the job counts as done. A process the app stopped is never mistaken for success, not even a playlist that had already saved files. Paused downloads can't be removed from the list; cancel them first.

**Keeping the queue across restarts.** Every time the list of unfinished downloads (waiting, running or paused) changes, it is saved to `data/queue.json`, together with whether each one was paused, how far it got, and the folder it started in. So a crash, a closed console window or a power cut loses nothing. Quitting the app pauses running downloads, keeping their partial files, and the quit question says they'll continue where they stopped. At start-up, downloads that were running come back and continue; downloads the user paused stay paused. Saved downloads are restored before the server answers any request, so they keep their order and nothing added in the meantime can overwrite them. The start-up cleanup of old partial folders runs alongside and skips the folders of saved downloads, however long they have been paused. A job's folder is saved as soon as it starts, and quitting waits for the final save of `queue.json`. Finished downloads go to the history as before; failed and cancelled ones are not kept.

**Download later.** **🌙 Later** next to Download (and next to Download all) adds the job as `scheduled` with `startsAt`, the next start time from Settings (`download-window.ts`). The rules:

- **Starting:** a waiting job starts once `startsAt` has passed and the window is open. With a stop time, the window is the time between the two (it may cross midnight); without one, it is always open once the start time has passed. Clicking **Later** while the window is open starts it right away.
- **Stopping:** at the stop time, "Later" jobs that are still waiting for a slot or downloading go back to `scheduled` with the next start time. They stop the way **Pause** does, so they keep their partial folder and continue where they stopped (shown as "Continues tomorrow at 02:00").
- **Start now**, **Resume** and **Retry** mean "now": the job leaves the window and runs until it finishes.
- **Checking the clock:** while any "Later" job is unfinished, the queue checks every 30 seconds, and also whenever the queue changes. Checking the wall clock rather than setting a timer for the start time keeps it right after sleep, hibernation or a clock change.
- **Changing the times:** jobs that are already due start first, then the waiting ones move to the new time, so saving any setting just after the start time can't push them to the next day. That pass runs again afterwards, because the new times can make waiting jobs due at once. Clearing the start time starts them all.
- **Settings page:** a time is saved when you leave its box, not on every change, so a half-typed time is never saved. An emptied start box gets its saved time back, with a hint, because an empty start time would start every waiting download. Only **Turn off** turns the times off, and with downloads waiting it asks first, since they then start at once. The stop box is disabled until a start time is saved, because a stop time alone does nothing.
- **Quitting:** with downloads waiting and none running, the desktop app asks before quitting, because they only start while it is open. When downloads are also running, the usual question adds a line saying the waiting ones wait too.
- **The message after clicking Later** comes from the job the server returns: "It starts tomorrow at 02:00", or, when the window is already open, that it started now. The buttons are disabled before the settings check, so a double click adds one download.
- **Restarts:** waiting jobs are saved with `later` and `startsAt` in `queue.json`. If the app was closed at the start time, they start when it opens inside the window, or without a stop time whenever it opens; otherwise they wait for the next start, and their `startsAt` moves there so the list shows the right day.

**Trimming a clip.** For a single video (not a live stream), the media panel can download only part of it. Times are typed as `1:23`, `1:02:03` or seconds, or taken from where the preview is paused. yt-dlp gets `--download-sections "*<start>-<end>"`; for video also `--force-keyframes-at-cuts`, which re-encodes around the cuts so the clip starts on the exact frame rather than the nearest keyframe (measured: a 10-second 1080p clip is exactly 10.000 s and takes about 10 s to make). The file name ends with the range, start rounded down and end rounded up (`Title (1m23s-4m57s).mp3`). yt-dlp reports progress for a section only once it is done, so the job shows "Downloading the clip" until then. It also reports the finished file twice when cutting, so a job ignores a file it already has.

**Picking videos from a playlist.** A playlist opens with every video ticked in a checklist (the first 500 are listed). The page sends the ticked positions as `playlistItems`, and yt-dlp gets them as `--playlist-items`, with runs written as ranges (`1-3,7,9-12`). With every listed video ticked, the page sends `null` instead, so a playlist longer than 500 still downloads completely. Positions count unavailable videos too, because yt-dlp's numbering does; the files keep their playlist number (`004 - Title`).

**Playlists with unavailable videos.** yt-dlp skips a private or removed video and carries on, but then exits with an error code. When a playlist saved at least one file, the job counts as done, and `Job.warning` says how many videos were skipped and why (for example "2 videos could not be downloaded. This video is private."). Only a playlist that saved nothing is a failure.

**Partial folders.** The shared `.yt-downloader-partial` folder is marked hidden on Windows, where a leading dot doesn't hide anything. At start-up, job folders left by a crash or a killed app are removed once nothing in them has changed for six hours. The delay is because another copy of the app (the browser mode, say) may be downloading into the same folder.

**Abandoned requests.** Reading a link and searching stop their yt-dlp process when the page stops waiting. The page aborts the previous request when a new link or search replaces it, and the server notices the closed connection.
