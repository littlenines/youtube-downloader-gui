# Downloads

1. The page sends `POST /api/downloads` with the user's choices.
2. `normalizeDownloadRequest` validates them into `DownloadOptions`, and the queue creates a `Job`.
3. When a slot is free (Settings → downloads at the same time), the queue waits for the tools and starts yt-dlp with `buildDownloadArguments`. The link always comes after `--`, so it can never be read as an option.
4. Each job gets its own partial folder, `<download folder>/.yt-downloader-partial/<job id>`, passed as `-P temp:`. yt-dlp keeps everything unfinished there (stream parts, fragments, thumbnails, merge and conversion output) and moves each file to the download folder only once it is complete.
5. yt-dlp prints marker lines that the app reads (`parseOutputLine`):
   - `VITEM`: an item starts, with its position among the videos being downloaded (`playlist_autonumber`: 2 of 2 when videos 1 and 3 were picked), the count, and the title. Files keep the position in the whole playlist (`playlist_index`, `003 - Title`).
   - `VPROG`: progress, with the stream's video and audio codec, bytes, total, speed and seconds left.
   - `VFILE`: the final file after merging and conversion.
6. The codecs tell the streams apart: picture only, sound only, both in one stream, or neither (subtitles). For a video, the picture fills the progress bar to 85% and the sound the rest. For a playlist, each video fills its share of the bar, so the second of four goes from 25% to 50%.

**What the list shows.** A download spends most of its time outside the actual download, so each job has an `activity` (`job-progress.ts`), and the list shows it:

| Activity | When | Shown |
|---|---|---|
| `preparing` | Waiting for the tools, reading the video page (3–8 s on YouTube, while yt-dlp solves its checks), and between a video's picture and sound | The step ("Getting the video info…") and a moving bar |
| `downloading` | A `VPROG` line arrived for a stream yt-dlp doesn't report as `finished` yet | The step, percent, "2.0 MB of 4.0 MB", speed and time left |
| `processing` | yt-dlp reported the last stream as `finished` (`progress.status`, the last field of `VPROG`). Not "downloaded ≥ total", because the total can be an estimate that a stream outgrows: converting or saving the audio (named per format, for example "Converting to MP3"), merging picture and sound, or a clip (whose progress yt-dlp only reports at the end, so a new item doesn't replace "Downloading the clip") | The step and a moving bar, without the last speed |

Music often downloads in a fraction of a second, faster than the list refreshes (every 0.7 s), while reading the page and converting take several seconds each, so the moving bar is what keeps those phases from looking stuck. A finished job also shows how long it took ("took 12s"): `activeSeconds` adds up each run, from `runStartedAt` to its end, so time spent paused or waiting for the next "Later" window doesn't count, and it's saved in `queue.json` across restarts. Progress that arrives after Pause or Cancel, while yt-dlp is still exiting, is ignored, and cancelling clears the activity, speed and time left. Subtitles clear the previous stream's numbers and show the moving bar. The moving bar sweeps across; with reduced motion turned on in the system, it pulses instead.
7. When the job ends in any way, its partial folder is deleted. On success the job is added to the history. On cancel the process tree is stopped (process group on Linux and macOS, `taskkill /T` on Windows), so nothing unfinished reaches the download folder at any stage.

A job keeps its slot until its yt-dlp process has exited, even after it was cancelled. Retrying a cancelled job queues it, and it starts only once the previous run has fully ended, so two runs never write the same file.

**Notifications.** The desktop app shows a system notification when a download finishes or fails while its window isn't focused (Settings → **Notify me when downloads finish**, on by default; desktop only, so the setting is hidden in browser mode):

- **Finding finished downloads:** `download-watcher.ts` checks the queue once a second and remembers the `finishedAt` it has reported for each job. A done or failed job with a different `finishedAt` is new, so even a download that starts and ends between two checks is noticed, a retried one is reported again, and a clock moved backwards can't repeat old notifications. Cancelled ones get no notification. Each listener (taskbar, notifications) runs on its own, so one that fails doesn't stop the other.
- **What it says** (`notification-text.ts`): the title and "Downloaded · MP3 · 320 kbps", or "Download failed: <reason>". A playlist gets one notification, named after it ("12 of 14 videos downloaded, 2 failed"): `newlyFinishedJobs` holds back a playlist's videos until none of them is still unfinished, then reports them together. A video retried later reports its whole playlist again, so the count stays right.
- **One notification for a batch** (`notification-batch.ts`): finishes are collected until 10 seconds pass without a new one, then shown together ("10 downloads finished", "9 downloaded, 1 failed", or "3 downloads failed"). So the several-links panel, a subscription check or an overnight **Later** queue sends one notification, not one per download, and a single download is shown 10 seconds after it finishes. Only finishes that happen while the window isn't focused are collected, and the window, the setting and support are checked again before showing.
- **Clicking it** brings the window back. The last 20 notifications are kept referenced, so the click still works after a while.
- **Windows** needs a Start Menu shortcut with the app's ID (`app.setAppUserModelId`) to show notifications, and Electron creates one the first time a notification is shown, named after the executable ("YT Downloader"). Windows names the notifications after that shortcut. Running from source (`npm run dev`) uses its own ID, "YT Downloader (development)", because otherwise its shortcut, named "Electron" after `electron.exe`, would carry the release app's ID and Windows could label the real app's notifications "Electron". Do Not Disturb, which Windows 11 also turns on by itself during full-screen apps and games, keeps the popup back; the notification still goes to the notification list (Win + N).

**Mini mode.** A small bar, always on top of other apps, that shows only the download progress while you work elsewhere (desktop only). The button next to ⚙ Settings opens it; Settings → **Switch to mini mode when minimizing** (off by default) also opens it when the window is minimized.

- **A separate window** (`mini-window.ts`): 360×84, frameless, always on top, not resizable, and the main window is hidden while it's open. Shrinking the main window instead doesn't work, because Electron can't remove a window's frame after it was made, and Windows' title bar would take most of an 84-pixel strip.
- **Its own small page,** `mini.html` with its own bundle `mini.js` (`src/client/mini.ts`, about 6 KB), listed in `WEB_ASSETS` like the main page. The bar's markup is in the HTML, so nothing of the full app loads or flashes in it. It polls `/api/downloads` like the downloads list (every second while something downloads, every 4 s otherwise), so there's one source for the numbers.
- **The hidden full window polls less:** the downloads list polls every 4 s instead of every 0.7 s while its page is hidden (`document.hidden`: the window is hidden for mini mode, minimized, or fully covered), and refreshes at once when it shows again.
- **What it shows** (`miniStatus` in `mini-status.ts`): the title of what is downloading, rotating every 4 s through several, with a playlist named once; a bar with the average progress of those downloads, a playlist counted as one with its finished songs as full, so the bar matches what it names (the taskbar also counts queued downloads, as an overall measure of the queue, which would hold the bar far below the song it names); and their total speed, the longest time left and how many are waiting. While everything running is in a step without a percent (getting the video info, converting, merging: `isWorkingWithoutProgress` in `@shared/jobs.ts`, shared with the downloads list), the bar moves instead of standing full, and a single download names the step ("Converting to MP3…"). Without that, converting a long compilation showed a full bar with no details for minutes, which looked finished or stuck. With nothing downloading it says how many are waiting or paused, "All downloads finished" with how many were done and failed, or "Nothing is downloading". The finished notification comes as usual, since the bar is rarely the focused window.
- **Going back:** the bar's button, a double-click on its text, or closing it in any other way (Alt+F4) closes the bar and shows the main window again, restored if it was minimized. A notification click and starting the app a second time do the same.
- **Dragging:** the grip and the bar's edges move the window (`app-region: drag`); the text and the button don't (`no-drag`). Windows treats a drag area like a title bar, so a double-click there never reaches the page, and the text has to stay outside it for the double-click to work.
- **Its position** is saved to `data/mini-window.json` when it is moved or closed, and used again if the bar would still be fully on a screen; otherwise it opens in the bottom-right corner of the main screen.
- **The taskbar** progress goes to one window: the bar once it is on screen (its own taskbar button, while the main one is hidden), otherwise the main window. Only the main window flashes when a download finishes, also when it is minimized: the bar already says so on screen, and since it never takes focus, a flashed bar button would stay highlighted until clicked. A window that comes back, such as the main window after mini mode, gets the progress again, because its taskbar button is new.
- **It doesn't take the keyboard focus** when it appears (`showInactive`): switching to mini mode on minimize happens while you start working in another app, and typing there must not go to the bar. A second request while it is still loading waits for it, rather than showing an empty bar.
- **Dropping a link on it** does nothing (`dragover` and `drop` are cancelled, with the no-drop cursor). Otherwise Chromium would navigate to the link, and the navigation guard would open it in the browser.
- **If its page fails to load,** the bar closes and the main window comes back, rather than both staying open. A cancelled load (`ERR_ABORTED`, for example Ctrl+R while it loads) doesn't count.
- **Safety:** it uses the main window's settings (`pageWebPreferences`: sandbox, context isolation, no Node) and navigation guard, and the main process answers `enter-mini-mode` and `leave-mini-mode` only from the app's own address, like the folder picker.

**Reusing the media panel's read.** Opening a video in the media panel already runs `yt-dlp -J`, which reads the page, the player and solves YouTube's checks. Each such read (not live streams) is kept for 30 minutes, up to 20 of them (`video-info-cache.ts`; about 150 KB for a song, up to about 0.9 MB for a talk with auto-generated captions, whose list of 160+ caption languages makes up most of it, so at most about 18 MB in all). When a single-video download starts from that link, the job runner writes the read into the job's partial folder as `video-info.json`, and yt-dlp gets `--load-info-json <file>` instead of `-- <link>`, so it skips straight to choosing formats and downloading:

- **Measured:** with the saved read, the download itself starts after about 0.6 s. Without it, yt-dlp first spends several seconds reading the video again; in the app that made each download 2–3 s slower, and up to 7 s when YouTube answered slowly.
- **Not used for** playlists, subscriptions, the several-links panel, or **Later** downloads that start after the 30 minutes, which have no recent read; they read the link as before.
- **30 minutes**, because YouTube's stream links expire after about 6 hours.
- **If a saved read fails before anything was downloaded** (an expired link, say), the read is forgotten and the download runs once more from the link, with its step and progress reset. A failure after the download started (a full disk, a failed merge) is not retried, since reading the link again wouldn't help. There is only one extra attempt.
- **If the read can't be written** to the partial folder, the download simply uses the link.
- **Pause and Cancel:** the stop flag is checked again right before yt-dlp starts, because writing the read takes a moment, and a Pause in that moment would otherwise be lost.
- **When yt-dlp updates itself,** every saved read is forgotten, since the new version may read videos differently.
- **The cache** is an `ExpiringMap` (`lib/expiring-map.ts`), the same helper that keeps preview links for an hour.

**Pause all and Resume all.** Above the list, **Pause all** shows when two or more downloads are running or queued, and **Resume all** when two or more are paused. A single download has its own buttons. Pause all pauses every running or queued download, so a queued one doesn't start as soon as a slot frees up. That includes **Later** downloads running in their time. Downloads still waiting for their **Later** time (`scheduled`) take no slot and keep waiting. All downloads are paused first and the queue is checked once, so a queued download is never started just to be stopped again. Resume all queues them oldest first, and they start as **Simultaneous downloads** allows. Unlike a single **Resume**, which means "now", Resume all keeps a **Later** download's times: before the stop time it continues, after it, it waits for the next start. With nothing to pause or resume, both do nothing, not even save the queue.

**Pausing and resuming.** **Pause** stops a download's yt-dlp process but keeps its partial folder, and the job becomes `paused`, which frees its slot. **Resume** queues it again: yt-dlp continues the `.part` files in the same partial folder instead of starting over (measured: a 4K download paused at 7% resumed at 7%). A clip is the exception, because its ffmpeg cut can't continue and starts again. A job remembers the download folder it started in, so changing the folder in Settings while it is paused doesn't lose its partial files. Partial files are deleted when a job is finished (done, failed or cancelled), or when its run was cancelled even if Retry was clicked before the process exited, so a retry always starts fresh. Pausing then resuming while the old process is still exiting keeps them. If yt-dlp finishes on its own just as Pause is clicked, the job counts as done. A process the app stopped is never mistaken for success, not even a playlist that had already saved files. Paused downloads can't be removed from the list; cancel them first.

**Keeping the queue across restarts.** Every time the list of unfinished downloads (waiting, running or paused) changes, it is saved to `data/queue.json`, together with whether each one was paused, how far it got, and the folder it started in. So a crash, a closed console window or a power cut loses nothing. Quitting the app pauses running downloads, keeping their partial files, and the quit question says they'll continue where they stopped. At start-up, downloads that were running come back and continue; downloads the user paused stay paused. Saved downloads are restored before the server answers any request, so they keep their order and nothing added in the meantime can overwrite them. The start-up cleanup of old partial folders runs alongside and skips the folders of saved downloads, however long they have been paused. A job's folder is saved as soon as it starts, and quitting waits for the final save of `queue.json`. Finished downloads go to the history as before; failed and cancelled ones are not kept.

**Download later.** **Later** next to Download (and next to Download all) adds the job as `scheduled` with `startsAt`, the next start time from Settings (`download-window.ts`). The rules:

- **Starting:** a waiting job starts once `startsAt` has passed and the window is open. With a stop time, the window is the time between the two (it may cross midnight); without one, it is always open once the start time has passed. Clicking **Later** while the window is open starts it right away.
- **Stopping:** at the stop time, "Later" jobs that are still waiting for a slot or downloading go back to `scheduled` with the next start time. They stop the way **Pause** does, so they keep their partial folder and continue where they stopped (shown as "Continues tomorrow at 02:00").
- **Start now**, **Resume** and **Retry** mean "now": the job leaves the window and runs until it finishes.
- **Checking the clock:** while any "Later" job is unfinished, the queue checks every 30 seconds, and also whenever the queue changes. Checking the wall clock rather than setting a timer for the start time keeps it right after sleep, hibernation or a clock change.
- **Changing the times:** jobs that are already due start first, then the waiting ones move to the new time, so saving any setting just after the start time can't push them to the next day. That pass runs again afterwards, because the new times can make waiting jobs due at once. Clearing the start time starts them all.
- **Settings page:** a time is saved when you leave its box, not on every change, so a half-typed time is never saved. An emptied start box gets its saved time back, with a hint, because an empty start time would start every waiting download. Only **Turn off** turns the times off, and with downloads waiting it asks first, since they then start at once. The stop box is disabled until a start time is saved, because a stop time alone does nothing.
- **Quitting:** with downloads waiting and none running, the desktop app asks before quitting, because they only start while it is open. When downloads are also running, the usual question adds a line saying the waiting ones wait too.
- **The message after clicking Later** comes from the job the server returns: "It starts tomorrow at 02:00", or, when the window is already open, that it started now. The buttons are disabled before the settings check, so a double click adds one download.
- **Restarts:** waiting jobs are saved with `later` and `startsAt` in `queue.json`. If the app was closed at the start time, they start when it opens inside the window, or without a stop time whenever it opens; otherwise they wait for the next start, and their `startsAt` moves there so the list shows the right day.

**Trimming a clip.** For a single video (not a live stream), the media panel can download only part of it. Times are typed as `1:23`, `1:02:03` or seconds, or taken from where the preview is paused. yt-dlp gets `--download-sections "*<start>-<end>"` and `--force-keyframes-at-cuts`, so the clip starts at the exact time. Without it, yt-dlp copies the stream and has to start at the seek point before the start time: a video at the keyframe before it, and audio too, which is easy to miss. Measured against the whole song, an MP3 clip of 1:00–1:10 started at 0:50 and was 20 s long; with it, it starts at 0:59.992 and is 10.03 s (Original 0:59.992, M4A 0:59.964, all 10 s), made in about 9 s. A 10-second 1080p video clip is exactly 10.000 s and takes about 10 s to make.

**The cut is re-encoded, so the clip gets quality settings.** For an exact cut, yt-dlp drops `-c copy` and lets ffmpeg encode the section with its defaults: about 100 kbps for YouTube's Opus audio and 129 kbps for its AAC. Every audio format is converted from that, so an "MP3 320" or FLAC clip was really built from a 96k file. So the cut gets `--downloader-args "ffmpeg_o:-b:a 320k"` (measured: 329 kbps Opus, 331 kbps AAC). For audio clips also `-c:v copy`: when a site has no audio-only stream, the picture would otherwise be encoded with x264 just to be thrown away when the sound is extracted. The clip still goes through one extra lossy encode at 320 kbps, which is hard to hear. **Opus · original** and **Original audio (no conversion)** clips are not the untouched stream, unlike whole downloads. Trimming in the conversion step instead was ruled out: a copied section starts at an unknown seek point up to about 10 s early, so there is nothing exact to trim from without downloading the whole track first. The file name ends with the range, start rounded down and end rounded up (`Title (1m23s-4m57s).mp3`). yt-dlp reports progress for a section only once it is done, so the job shows "Downloading the clip" until then. It also reports the finished file twice when cutting, so a job ignores a file it already has.

**Picking videos from a playlist.** A playlist opens with every video ticked in a checklist (the first 500 are listed). The page sends the ticked positions as `playlistItems`. With every listed video ticked, the page sends `null` instead, so a playlist longer than 500 still downloads completely. Positions count unavailable videos too, because yt-dlp's numbering does.

**A playlist downloads as one download per video** (`playlist-jobs.ts`), so each video can be paused, cancelled or retried on its own:

- **Listing the videos.** The server needs every video's link. Reading a playlist in the media panel already lists all of them (`--flat-playlist`), not just the 500 the page shows, so `playlist-videos.ts` remembers that listing for 30 minutes, like a video's read. Clicking Download then adds the videos at once (measured: 4 ms for a 14-video album). After the 30 minutes, or from the API without a read, the server reads the playlist again first.
- **One job per picked video,** with `playlist: false`, the video's page link (`webpage_url` before `url`, which for a fully read entry is the media file itself), `playlistTitle` and `playlistNumber`. All of them share a `group` (`id`, playlist `title`, `size`, the number of videos picked, and the playlist's `url`), saved in `queue.json`.
- **File names: just the song.** A song is saved as `<playlist>/Title.mp3`, without a playlist number in front (users don't want `004 - ` in their music library). The number is only shown in the list, for the order. Two songs with exactly the same title in one playlist would get the same name, so yt-dlp skips the second as already downloaded. A playlist downloaded as one job (below) is saved the same way.
- **The playlist folder is named by yt-dlp,** exactly as `%(playlist_title)s` used to name it: the job has no playlist when it runs (`--no-playlist`), so `--parse-metadata` puts the title into a `playlist_folder` field, and the output path uses `%(playlist_folder)s/`. Field values go through yt-dlp's own file name rules (measured: `Discovery: Live? / AC\DC "Best" 100%` becomes `Discovery Live  ACDC Best 100%` both ways). With `folder` set, the playlist folder goes inside it, as before. A playlist downloaded as one job uses the same field when its title is known.
- **A playlist name can't lead elsewhere.** yt-dlp keeps a title of `..` as `..`, so a playlist named that way saved into the folder above the download folder (measured), and `CON`, a name Windows refuses, made every song fail. `safePlaylistTitle` drops a title that is only dots, spaces and path characters (the song then saves directly in the download folder) and turns a Windows device name into `CON_`, before yt-dlp sees it.
- **Unusual playlists keep the old way.** If a picked entry is a playlist itself, or has no web link (some sites list only ids), the playlist is queued as one job, as before, and yt-dlp handles it. So no video is silently left out.
- **One history entry per playlist.** A playlist's songs write no history entries of their own. When none of them is unfinished any more, the playlist gets one entry with its id, all its files and "· playlist (12 videos)", written again (replacing it) if a retried song finishes later. A 300-song playlist therefore doesn't push every earlier download out of the 300-entry history, and removing the playlist removes one entry.
- **Counts survive a restart.** `queue.json` keeps a playlist's finished songs (status, files, size, error) until the whole playlist is finished, so after a restart the header still says "30 of 50 done", and the notification counts every song. For the same reason a finished song inside an unfinished playlist has no ✕, and **Clear finished** leaves it until the playlist is done; the playlist's own ✕ appears once everything is finished.
- **`queue.json` is written at most every 30 seconds** (`throttleSaves` in `lib/throttled-save.ts`): the first change at once, later changes together with the latest state when 30 seconds have passed, and at once when the app quits. Every song's start and finish changes the queue, and every save holds every song, so a 500-song playlist used to write about 486 MB and a 3000-song one about 17.6 GB (measured: 0.5 and 3 MB per save, twice per song). If the app crashes, at most the last 30 seconds are lost: a song finished in them is queued again, and yt-dlp finds its file already there.
- **The taskbar** counts a playlist as one download, with its finished songs as full, like the playlist header. Averaging the songs that are running or queued kept the bar near 0% for a long playlist.
- **They follow the queue like any download:** as many at once as **Simultaneous downloads** allows (a playlist used to download one video at a time), and **Later** applies to each.
- **The list** (`download-groups.ts`, `download-group-rows.ts`) shows the playlist once, at the place of its newest video, as a header with "5 of 14 videos done · 1 failed · 2 downloading", a bar averaging its videos (finished ones count as full), and **Pause**, **Resume** and **Cancel** for the whole playlist (named without "all", so they don't read like the list's own **Pause all**). Its videos follow in playlist order, numbered, each with its own buttons. The arrow at the right of the header folds them away and back, like an accordion (`aria-expanded`, with "Hide videos" or "Show videos" as its label); that is remembered by the page only, and re-draws the last answer instead of asking the server again. A playlist of more than 50 videos starts folded.
- **Long playlists** were measured with fake ones in the real page, also with the CPU slowed 4× and 6× (Chromium's `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`, as DevTools does for low-end devices). Every row of an unfolded playlist cost work on every poll: on a 6× slower CPU, 3000 songs blocked the page for 2.2 s of every 10 s, with freezes of up to 1.3 s, and the list fell behind (4 polls in 10 s instead of 14). 500 songs cost about as much as the page's own start-up. So:
  - A playlist of more than 50 videos starts folded.
  - An unfolded playlist shows its first 100 videos (`VIDEOS_SHOWN_AT_ONCE`), then a row with "2900 more videos not shown", **Show 100 more** and **Show all**. Songs that are downloading always show too, after the first 100, so the ones you may want to pause or cancel never hide behind the cap; there are only as many as **Simultaneous downloads**. Paused and failed songs past the cap are counted in the header, and Show all reaches them (after a Pause all there can be thousands, so they don't bypass the cap). The row's count is redrawn whenever it changes. With the cap, 3000 songs on a 6× slower CPU block the page for 1.3 s of every 10 s, with freezes of 0.4 s at most, about the page's own start-up, and the list keeps up (9 polls). What is left is parsing every poll: it sends every job, 2.2 MB every 0.7 s for 3000 songs. Sending only what changed would be the next step.
  - Row thumbnails load only when near the screen (`loading="lazy"`: 19 requests instead of 3000). Each thumbnail keeps its size and grey background while it loads, so nothing moves.
  - A virtual list (drawing only the rows in view) wasn't needed: the cap bounds the rows, and a virtual list wouldn't save the poll parsing.
- **Older saved playlists,** queued as one job by an earlier version, still download as one job with `--playlist-items`, now without numbers in the file names.

**Playlists with unavailable videos.** yt-dlp skips a private or removed video and carries on, but then exits with an error code. When a playlist saved at least one file, the job counts as done, and `Job.warning` says how many videos were skipped and why (for example "2 videos could not be downloaded. This video is private."). Only a playlist that saved nothing is a failure.

**Partial folders.** The shared `.yt-downloader-partial` folder is marked hidden on Windows, where a leading dot doesn't hide anything. At start-up, job folders left by a crash or a killed app are removed once nothing in them has changed for six hours. The delay is because another copy of the app (the browser mode, say) may be downloading into the same folder.

**Abandoned requests.** Reading a link and searching stop their yt-dlp process when the page stops waiting. The page aborts the previous request when a new link or search replaces it, and the server notices the closed connection.
